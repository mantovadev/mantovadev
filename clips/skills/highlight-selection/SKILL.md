# highlight-selection

## Trigger

An event has a compact transcript (`clips/work/<event>/transcript.json`, from
`transcript-extract`) and needs a short list of clip candidates for a human to
choose from before anything gets cut.

## What it does

An agent (you) reads the whole talk, picks the best 5 moments by segment index
and exact quote (never by timestamp), and writes them to
`clips/work/<event>/highlights.json`. Code (`snap_clip.py`) then validates
those picks, computes an actual cut point for each one (snapped to detected
silence where possible), flags problems (too short, too long, quote mismatch,
overlap), and writes a human-readable review sheet (`candidates.md`). A human
picks which candidates actually get cut; only then does `choose` mark them.

You never compute timestamps or do time arithmetic. Segment times are
reliable; word times are rough (see `transcript-extract`'s SKILL.md timing
contract). All of that is handled by `snap_clip.py`, which works from segment
indices and silence detection, not from anything you calculate.

## Inputs

- `clips/work/<event>/transcript.json`: segments (`i`, `s`, `e`, `text`) and
  words (`w`, `s`, `e`, `seg`). Segment `i` is the index you reference.
- `clips/work/<event>/audio.wav`: used by `snap_clip.py` for silence detection.
  You do not touch this file directly.

## Outputs

- `clips/work/<event>/highlights.json`: see `clips/schemas/highlights.schema.json`.
  You write the `event`, `source_transcript` and `candidates[]` (each with `id`,
  `start_seg`, `end_seg`, `start_quote`, `end_quote`, `score`, `hook`, `reason`,
  `needs_visuals`). `snap_clip.py` adds `cut` and `chosen` to each candidate;
  never write those fields yourself.
- `clips/work/<event>/candidates.md`: generated by `snap_clip.py`, a plain
  review sheet for the human (time range, flags, hook, reason, transcript text,
  preview file).
- `clips/work/<event>/previews/candidate_<id>.mp4`: generated by
  `snap_clip.py preview`. Small review files: sentence captions burned in, 5 s
  of padding before and after the cut, labelled "OUTSIDE CUT" while it plays.

## How

1. Run `clips/skills/highlight-selection/render_transcript.sh --event
   <event>` and read the **entire** output, start to finish, before picking
   anything. It prints one numbered line per segment: `[<i>] <mm:ss> <text>`.
   `<i>` is the segment index you will reference; do not reuse the `<mm:ss>`
   stamp for anything, it is only there to help you navigate while reading.

2. Identify where the actual talk starts and ends. Ignore:
   - organisational/logistics intro (welcome, sponsors, housekeeping) before
     the speaker gets into the subject matter;
   - audience Q&A where the question itself is inaudible or garbled in the
     transcript (the answer alone is rarely self-contained);
   - closing logistics (thanks, next event, apericena).

3. Pick the best **5** candidates from what remains, ranked 1 (best) to 5. The
   human will typically keep only 2 or 3, so rank honestly instead of padding.
   Spread them across the talk. Do not cluster all 5 near the beginning just
   because that is what you read first.

   Criteria, in rough priority order:
   - Self-contained: makes sense with zero prior context from earlier in the
     talk. If it needs a setup, include the setup segment(s) or drop it.
   - Has a hook: a strong, surprising or counterintuitive claim, a concrete
     number, or a short story/anecdote. "Interesting but flat" explanation is
     not enough on its own.
   - Works without seeing the slides. Set `needs_visuals` honestly; prefer
     candidates where it is `false`, but do not force it false if the segment
     truly depends on a chart or code on screen.
   - Opening: the first segment must make sense to someone who heard nothing
     before it. Reject an opening that is the tail of the previous thought,
     that has a dangling subject or pronoun (e.g. a sentence starting with
     "Ha consumato ..." where the viewer cannot know what "it" is), or that
     starts with a connective pointing backwards ("Infatti", "Però", "Quindi",
     "Cioè", "Allora") unless it is a complete rhetorical question that works
     on its own.
   - Ending: before fixing `end_seg`, read the two or three segments after it;
     if the real punchline lives there, include it. When a span runs too
     long, cut setup from the start rather than the payoff from the end. The
     `hook` line can supply missing context (it may be shown as on-screen
     text), so prefer a tight clip with a strong ending over a complete setup.
   - Garbled transcript: if the last or first sentence of a candidate is
     clearly mis-transcribed nonsense, prefer boundaries that avoid it, and
     mention the transcription doubt in `reason` so the human checks the
     preview.
   - Length: hard limits are `too_short` under 15 s and `too_long` over 60 s.
     Snapping can add up to about 1.5 s at the end and 0.35 s at the start, so
     aim for 25 to 50 s by the `<mm:ss>` stamps and leave at least 5 s of
     margin under 60 (do not do exact arithmetic; a rough glance at the
     stamps is enough). Do not max out the length: shorter and punchier beats
     a clip that drags to 59 s.
   - Candidates must not overlap in segment range with each other.

4. For each candidate, write:
   - `start_seg` / `end_seg`: the segment indices (inclusive) that bound the
     clip.
   - `start_quote` / `end_quote`: copy the **exact text** of the first and
     last segment from the rendered transcript, character for character
     (these are checked against `transcript.json` as a checksum: if you
     paraphrase or fix a typo, the check will fail).
   - `score` (0-100): your confidence this is a good standalone clip.
   - `hook`: one line, in **Italian**, that could open the clip as a social
     post (e.g. a question or a bold claim).
   - `reason`: one or two sentences, in **English**, on why this works as a
     clip.
   - `needs_visuals`: boolean.

   Write `clips/work/<event>/highlights.json` matching this skeleton:

   ```json
   {
     "event": "2026-09-17",
     "source_transcript": "clips/work/2026-09-17/transcript.json",
     "candidates": [
       {
         "id": 1,
         "start_seg": 42,
         "end_seg": 47,
         "start_quote": "<exact text of segment 42>",
         "end_quote": "<exact text of segment 47>",
         "score": 88,
         "hook": "<one Italian line>",
         "reason": "<why this works, in English>",
         "needs_visuals": false
       }
     ]
   }
   ```

5. Run:

   ```
   clips/skills/highlight-selection/snap_clip.py snap --event <event>
   ```

   This validates your candidates, computes `cut` for each, and writes
   `candidates.md`. Read its stdout summary and `candidates.md`.

6. Fix problems and re-run `snap`:
   - `quote_mismatch`: your `start_quote` or `end_quote` did not match the
     transcript (after trimming whitespace and lowercasing). Re-copy the exact
     text from `render_transcript.sh`'s output.
   - `too_short`: extend `end_seg` (or pull `start_seg` earlier) to include
     more of the thought, then re-run. Do not shrink the requirement instead.
   - `too_long`: narrow `start_seg`/`end_seg` to the tightest self-contained
     span, then re-run.
   - `overlaps_candidate_<id>`: adjust the segment ranges so candidates do not
     share segments.
   Re-run `snap` after every edit until no candidate has a blocking flag you
   can fix by adjusting segment ranges. `snap` is idempotent and safe to
   re-run as many times as needed; it always recomputes from your current
   `highlights.json`.

7. Render the review files:

   ```
   clips/skills/highlight-selection/snap_clip.py preview --event <event>
   ```

8. Review the candidates **with the human, one at a time**. You drive the
   session; never decide for the human which clips to keep.
   - First present a short summary list: id, score, duration, the hook, and
     one line of your own honest opinion per candidate (weak opening, ending
     cut before the punchline, garbled transcript, and so on).
   - Then, for each candidate in rank order: open its preview for the human
     (`open clips/work/<event>/previews/candidate_<id>.mp4` on macOS,
     `xdg-open` on Linux), say where the clip starts and ends in the player
     (it is printed in `candidates.md`), and wait for the verdict: keep, drop,
     or adjust.
   - The human gives times as read off the player. Apply them, which also
     re-renders that preview, then open it again and ask again:

     ```
     clips/skills/highlight-selection/snap_clip.py choose --event <event> --start 2=0:12 --end 2=0:58
     ```

     `--start` / `--end` take a player time (seconds or `M:SS`) and may point
     into the padding to extend the clip. `--shift-start ID=SECONDS` /
     `--shift-end ID=SECONDS` (negative = earlier) move an edge relative to the
     snapped cut instead; `ID=0` resets it. Adjustments are kept per candidate
     and per edge until replaced, so you can work through the clips one by one.
   - If the human wants a different span rather than a nudge (another
     sentence to start from, a punchline a few segments later), edit
     `start_seg` / `end_seg` and the quotes in `highlights.json`, re-run
     `snap` and `preview --ids <id>`, and review that candidate again. `snap`
     keeps the adjustments of every candidate whose span did not change; the
     candidate you re-spanned starts again from its snapped cut.
   - Move to the next candidate only when the human is done with the current
     one.

9. When every candidate has a verdict, record the final selection:

   ```
   clips/skills/highlight-selection/snap_clip.py choose --event <event> --ids 1,3
   ```

   `--ids` is the complete list of kept candidates (all others become
   unchosen); adjustments made in step 8 are preserved. This is the last step
   of this skill; cutting the clips is a separate step (`clip-cutter`).
